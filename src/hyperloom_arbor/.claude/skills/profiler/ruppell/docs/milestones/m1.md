## Programs

- **Probe** (`tests/probes/graph_probe.hip`): a graph of 8 distinct scoped
  kernels, captured once and replayed in 1000 steps, with 2 scoped eager
  kernels per step on a second stream, and a scoped setup step.
- **PyTorch demo** (`examples/torch_graph_demo.py`): two transformer-style
  blocks (LayerNorm, Linear, GELU, Linear, residual add) scoped by module
  hooks, captured with `torch.cuda.CUDAGraph` and replayed in 200 steps, with
  a scoped matmul on a second stream per step, plus setup, warmup and capture
  steps. torch 2.8.0+rocm7.0.2 (lightweight build on the system ROCm).

## Cost

Wall time of the probe with the tool off and on, three runs each at two
lengths (`overhead.txt`): about **259 ms fixed per process** (SDK and tool
start, flush, exit) and **2.5 µs per dispatch**. Measured on a probe that
launches back to back; a served model launches far fewer kernels per second
(the demo ran at about 4,000 dispatches/s). M3 measures it as a paired
per-request effect on the real workload, as the plan requires.

## How it differs from the roadmap's first sketch

- **Build**: `runtime/build.sh` (g++ for the tool, hipcc for the marker and
  probe), not CMake; enough for one platform. CMake comes with the CUDA
  backend.
- **Join**: position join only; the SDK reports no graph node id.
- **Witness**: `rocprofv3` in the same process, not a separate run. Separate
  runs could not agree within 3% on small kernels overlapping on two streams
  (up to 31% apart, job 47882).
- **Chrome export details learned on the way**: overlap lanes per queue,
  markers on their own track without flows, flows starting at the launching
  call's beginning.

## Limits

- The viewer checks import the files programmatically; nobody has looked at
  them in the UIs yet. The two traces are on the operator's workstation
  (`<laptop path>`) for a look in ui.perfetto.dev or
  `chrome://tracing` (enable internal debugging pages at
  `chrome://chrome-urls` first).
- One GPU, one process, one thread capturing. Graphs captured in global mode
  from several threads, and multi-rank runs, come with M2 and M3.
- No vLLM, no serving schema, no always-on census or out-of-process drain:
  those are M2 and M3.
